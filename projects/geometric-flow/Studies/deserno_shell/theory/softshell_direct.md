Run everything with `micromamba run -n mir_mem python <script>` (1-2 threads; a case takes 20-190 s).
Units: a = 1, kappa = KB/2, E~ = E/(pi kappa), w~ = 2 w a^2/kappa = 4 KI a^2/KB, sigma~ = sigma a^2/kappa = 2 KA a^2/KB.

## 1. The functional (derived from the C++)

What `main_cluster` minimises for one bead on the planar disk is (src/BeadGeometry.cpp `coverageShellWeight`, `evaluateFaceCoverage`;
src/Interaction.cpp `Adhesion::Face_Energy`; `Bending_tan`; `Excess_tension`)

    E = (KB/4) int (c1+c2)^2 dA   +   KA max(0, A - A0)   -   KI a^2 sum_{f: rhat_f . n_f < 0} w(r_f) |omega_f|

* bending: the code energy is KB H^2 with H = (c1+c2)/2, i.e. the Helfrich form (kappa/2)(2H)^2 with kappa = KB/2, zero spontaneous
  curvature, no Gaussian-curvature term;
* tension: `Excess_tension` = KA (A - A0) with A0 = initial area of the flat disk (the floor at A = A0 is never active for a deformed state),
  so the tension is the constant sigma = KA;
* adhesion: faces whose outward normal points towards the bead (`dot(radial, normal) < 0`, selection only, not differentiated) contribute
  w(r_f) |omega_f| where r_f is the distance from the bead centre to the face CENTROID, omega_f the exact solid angle of the triangle seen
  from the bead centre, and w(r) = [ (1 + cos pi x)/2 ]^p, x = (r - a)/(s a), |x| < 1 (zero outside).  p = 1 is the simulated shell
  (= cos^2(pi x/2), 1 only at r = a, symmetric, NO hard core: the membrane may enter r < a), p = 2, 4, 8 = cos^4, cos^8, cos^16 of pi x/2
  (`shell_power`).

Continuum limit and every approximation made:

1. **Mesh -> smooth surface.**  |omega_f| = int_f |n . rhat| dA / r^2 is exact for a planar triangle, so the sum over faces becomes
   `int W((r-a)/(s a)) |n.rhat|_+ a^2 dA / r^2 = a^2 int W dOmega` over the part of the surface that faces the bead.  The only
   approximation here is that the weight of a face is sampled at its centroid instead of being averaged over the face (error O(h^2 W''),
   NOT small for faces of the size of the shell: edge 0.25-0.5 against shell half width s a = 0.25 -- this is a mesh effect that the
   continuum model does not contain) and that the selection uses the face normal at the centroid (faces for which n . rhat changes sign
   inside the face have omega ~ 0 anyway, so the selection is continuous; it is not differentiated in the code).
   No occlusion: every part of the surface within the shell and facing the bead counts, also behind other parts (as in the code).
2. **Axisymmetry.**  Bead centre on the axis, membrane a surface of revolution generated by (rho(s), h(s)), tangent angle psi
   (rho' = cos psi, h' = sin psi), n = (-sin psi, cos psi) (left normal; points to the bead on a cap that hugs the bead from below, c1 = psi' = +1,
   c2 = sin psi / rho = +1 on the unit sphere).  Non-axisymmetric states are not considered; branches are followed quasi-statically.
3. **Edge.**  The simulation clamps the boundary vertices: in the surviving old run (`Wrapping_planar_excess/50`) the boundary ring of
   `Final_state.obj` has radius 10.0000 and x = +-1e-6 exactly as in `membrane_0.obj` (checked, read-only), and A - A0 = (excess) ~ 4 pi in
   the enveloped state (baseline B0), i.e. the disk keeps its projected area pi R^2.  The model therefore has rho(S) = R (default R = 10),
   h and the slope psi free (natural boundary condition: bead free to move along the axis; moment-free edge).  A0 = pi R^2, so
   A - A0 = int rho (1 - cos psi) ds * 2 pi = pure excess area, exactly Deserno's tension term; R -> infinity is Deserno's membrane.
4. **Reduction to one dimension** (E~ = E/(pi kappa), 2 pi rho ds = dA, dOmega = 2 pi rho ds |n.rhat| / r^2):

        E~ = int rho (c1 + c2)^2 ds  +  sigma~ * 2 int rho (1 - cos psi) ds  -  w~ G
        G  = int ds rho W((r-1)/s) b^+ / r^3 ,   b = rho sin psi - h cos psi = -r (n . rhat),  r = sqrt(rho^2 + h^2)

   (the adhesion prefactor: KI a^2 -> w~ kappa / 2 gives E_ad/(pi kappa) = -(w~/2pi) int W dOmega = -w~ G with G = (1/2pi) int W dOmega).
   G is exactly the quantity z_ad = -Bead/(2 pi KI) used in the baseline analysis of the simulation (adhesion-weighted covered solid
   angle / 2 pi); for s -> 0, G -> z = 1 - cos(alpha) of Deserno; a full unit sphere gives G = 2 (adhesion -2 w~, bending +8).
   s -> 0 check of the normalisation: a spherical cap of angle alpha gives bending 4 z, adhesion -w~ z, tension sigma~ z^2
   (excess area pi z^2): E~ = -(w~ - 4) z + sigma~ z^2 + F(z), as in Deserno.  (Verified numerically in the tests: unit-sphere cap G and bending.)

## 2. Discretisation and solvers

* **Variables.**  N segments with chord angles psi_i (i = 0..N-1) and FIXED length fractions ell_i, L_i = S ell_i; the clamp rho_N = R fixes
  S = R / sum(ell_i cos psi_i) EXACTLY, so the unknowns are (psi_0 .. psi_{N-1}, h_p) (h_p = height of the pole relative to the bead
  centre = position of the bead).  No tangential gauge freedom, no constraint handling.
* **Bending** (second order for smooth non-uniform grids): node sum rho_i lbar_i (c1 + c2)^2 with c1 = (psi_i - psi_{i-1})/lbar_i,
  lbar = (L_{i-1} + L_i)/2, c2 = sin(psibar_i)/rho_i with psibar the length-weighted interpolation of the adjacent chord angles.
  **Pole**: the node weight is rho_0 = 0, so without a correction a conical / catenoid-like tip (psi_0 ~ 0.6 on the first 1e-3 of arclength,
  c1 + c2 ~ 0 on the first node) was a spurious local minimum that lowered E by ~0.02 (found and fixed: the pole cell [0, L_0/2] is
  integrated with c1 = c2 = 2 psi_0/L_0, energy 2 psi_0^2).
* **Tension**: exact frustum areas, A_ex/pi = sum (rho_i + rho_{i+1}) L_i (1 - cos psi_i) (no cancellation of large numbers).
* **Adhesion**: 6-point Gauss-Legendre per segment (also tested with 10) of rho W b^+ / r^3 along the chord (b is constant on a chord, so the
  selection is per segment, continuous because the integrand carries the factor b^+).
* **Derivatives.**  Element energies are differentiated by complex step (exact), second derivatives by central differences of those
  (1e-9), chained analytically through the cumulative-sum map psi -> positions and the closure S(psi); gradient and the dense reduced
  Hessian were verified against finite differences (relative error < 1e-7 / 1e-6, tests [1]).
* **Fixed w~**: Newton with eigenvalue modification (|lambda| floored) + trust radius + Armijo line search.
* **Fixed coverage G (KKT)**: minimise E_el = E_bend + sigma~ A_ex at G = G_t; the Lagrange multiplier is w~ = d e/d G.  Bordered Newton
  (symmetric (N+2) system) with tangent predictor, continuation G0 -> G1.  Because e(G) does not depend on w~, ONE trace gives the whole
  phase behaviour: stationary states at any w~ are the roots of w~(G) = w~; S1 = first maximum of w~(G) (limit of stability of the
  partially wrapped state: a quasi-static increase of w~ must jump), S2 = the following minimum (limit of stability of the enveloped
  state), E = Maxwell (equal energy of the two stable minima).  Folds in w~ are no problem for a G-continuation.
* **Grid.**  Nodes are re-equidistributed along the curve (every second continuation step, and after each trace) with a spacing
  monitor 1/h = 1/h_base + |d f/ds| / df_tol + |dpsi/ds| / dpsi_tol (+ a neck term), f = rho W q / r^2 the adhesion density per arclength:
  dense where the weight changes (contact line, shell edges) and where the membrane bends, smooth (spacing grows <= 30 % per unit length).
  Grids: coarse / mid / fine / vfine (`GRIDS`), N ~ 100-300 / 150-350 / 300-550 / ~1000.
* **Lines.**  Scan on the `mid` grid; the extrema of w~(G) are re-converged on a FROZEN `fine` grid (no regridding => smooth w~(G)) and located by a
  polynomial fit over +-0.1 in G; E from fixed-w~ minimisations of both branches on the fine grid (brentq on E_partial - E_enveloped).

## 3. Results (all numbers from runs of this code; reproduce with `run_softshell_scan.py`, `compare_sim.py`, `analyze_s0.py`)

Notation: lines are quoted as S1 / E / S2 in w~ = 4 KI a^2/KB; "G" is the coverage z_ad of the simulation (G = 2 for the full sphere);
S1 = limit of stability of the partially wrapped state (the state a flat start follows while w~ is raised), S2 = limit of stability of
the enveloped state (the state a full start follows while w~ is lowered), E = equal energy of the two minima.  Both are read from the single
curve w~(G) = de/dG (see 2.): S1 = first maximum, S2 = following minimum, E by fixed-w~ minimisation of both branches.
The simulated lines (+-0.2 bracket) are those of `data/baseline_lines.csv`.

## 4. Limitations and what is not verified

* Axisymmetric, quasi-static, clamped edge (verified for one old run only, boundary radius 10.0000 in initial and final mesh): non-axisymmetric states and the dynamics of the BFGS/remeshing minimiser
  (which decides where a state is lost) are not modelled; S1 and S2 are the exact limits of stability of the continuum model (Hessian), the simulation can jump earlier by noise / mesh.
* The continuum model does not know the mesh: centroid sampling of the shell on faces of the size of the shell, discrete bending and the remeshing energy kick are not in it.  The remaining offsets
  (S2 +0.7, E +0.2, S1 +0.24 on average) are what is left for them but cannot be attributed to the mesh by this study alone.
* S2 (and G_S2) at sigma~ >= 1 carry ~0.02 numerical uncertainty (steep V); S1 at small sigma~ ~ 0.005-0.01; E ~ 0.003.  s = 0.003 could not be run (trace failed); s = 0.00625 needs the retry from a bound start
  (`initial_state_bound`) because the narrow-shell plate stays stable to w~ ~ 3-4.
* Hysteresis: S1 and S2 are followed along ONE continuation curve e(G), which holds because w~(G) is single valued for sigma~ <= 2 (the S-shaped regime of Deserno, sigma~ > 4.7, was not computed).
* The prefactor of the line-tension estimate is off by 1.5; only the sqrt(s_eff) scaling and the collapse are verified numerically.
